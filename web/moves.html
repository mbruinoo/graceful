Can this bracket move?

Lift the bracket off the line. The tree falls into two pieces. The step is allowed when the bracket's two feet would come down in different pieces.

Try it

Tap a bracket to lift it. The two pieces it was holding together are marked ● round and ■ square. Then look at where its feet would land one step to the left and one step to the right.

Left: the line with one bracket per length; the number at the left of each row is the bracket's length. Right: the same tree drawn as dots and lines, with the lifted bracket dashed and the step you are looking at drawn in green or red.

Why the test works

  1. A tree on these dots is connected and has no loops. Every bracket is the only link between the two pieces on either side of it.
  2. Take one bracket away and you have exactly two pieces, with no link between them.
  3. Put it back one step over. If one foot is in each piece, the pieces are linked again and there is still no loop: a tree.
  4. If both feet are in the same piece, that piece now has a loop and the other piece is cut off: not a tree.

The only other way a step can fail is by running out of line: a foot would land before the first dot or after the last.

Three things you can read off without colouring

A long bracket on a dead end is stuck

If one foot stands on a dot that nothing else touches, lifting the bracket leaves that dot as a piece all by itself. A foot would have to land back on that same dot, and a bracket of length 2 or more cannot do that in one step.

The shortest bracket pivots on a dead end

A bracket of length 1 with a foot on a dead-end dot can always swing to the other side of that dot, if there is room. Its other foot leaves, the dead-end foot stays put.

In the star only the shortest bracket moves

Every bracket of the star has its right foot on a dead end. So all the long ones are stuck, and the shortest one pivots. That is the only way out of the star.
