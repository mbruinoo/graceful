Two brackets at once

A step that is blocked on its own can be fine as half of a pair. The first step closes a loop and cuts a piece off. The second takes a bracket out of that loop and uses it to reach the piece that was cut off.

Try it

The two "stuck" positions have only one single step available, and it leads to a position with only the step back. With single steps you can never leave. Pairs get you out.

The same rule, in pieces

Lift one bracket and the tree falls into two pieces; the bracket must come down with a foot in each. Lift two brackets and it falls into three pieces. The two brackets must come down so that all three are tied together again: each joins two different pieces, and they do not both join the same two.

The loop picture above is this rule seen one step at a time. A loop means a bracket came down with both feet in one piece. The only repair is to take some other bracket out of that loop and put it down across the gap.

Why it matters

With single steps, the positions split into separate families that cannot reach each other. With pairs allowed as well, every position checked is in one family.

A caution. A pair passes through a position that is not a tree, so this is a looser game than the original one-step game. It tells us the isolated families are an artefact of taking steps one at a time. It does not by itself show that every tree shape can be reached.
