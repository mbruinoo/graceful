FAMILY
Spiders with one leg of length r and k legs of length 2 (m = r+2k), all legs zigzag: (A) every k >= 1 and every r with r+k ≡ 1 (mod 3), r >= (k-1)/2 for k odd, r >= k/2+1 for k even; (B) every k divisible by 3 and every r >= 2k (all three residues; the exact first r per residue is c-k). Extra legs of length 1 can be added freely (README section 13 reduction). NOT covered: k not divisible by 3 with r+k ≡ 0 or 2 (mod 3), and small r below the thresholds.

RULE
Top-block rule. B = r+k. A DESIGN for k is (c; delta_1..delta_k), distinct offsets >= 0. Pick the design with c ≡ B (mod 3); set l = (B-c)/3 (need l >= 0) and v = B-2l.
Short leg i (i=1..k): first number B+i (up to label B+i), second number v-delta_i (down to label 2l+i+delta_i).
Long leg: all remaining numbers of 1..B in decreasing order, alternately up, down, up, ...

Designs (closed form):
- k odd: c=(3k-1)/2; delta_i = k-2i for i <= (k-1)/2, delta_i = 2k-2i for i >= (k+1)/2.
- k=2h even: c=3h+1; delta_i = h+2+s for i = 2h-1-2s (s=0..h-1); delta_i = h-j for i = 2j (j=1..h).
Both have c ≡ 1 (mod 3), giving (A).
- k divisible by 3 (tower): q = k with all factors 2 and 3 removed, Q=3q, T=k/Q, d0 = the odd design for q. For t=0,1,2 take block offsets D=(0,q,2q), (0,q,4q), (0,3q,4q) and block permutation al=(1,2,0), (2,0,1), (1,2,0). Middle design for Q: mid(jq+i2) = D[al[j]] + d0(i2) (j=0,1,2; i2=1..q), c_mid = (3+t)q + (3q-1)/2. Final: delta(JQ+i) = 3Q(T-1-J) + mid(i) (J=0..T-1; i=1..Q), c = 3Q(T-1) + c_mid. Then c ≡ 1+tq (mod 3), so t=0,1,2 give all residues, giving (B).

Example r=10, k=3: long leg numbers 13 12 11 10 9 8 4 3 2 1 (labels 13 1 12 2 11 3 7 4 6 5); short legs 14,6 (labels 14, 8); 15,5 (15, 10); 16,7 (16, 9).

PROOF
Full text in /home/user/graceful/src/rules/twolens/PROOF.txt.

1. Lemma (zigzag with skips). Let D be k numbers in 1..B, v = max D, B-v = 2l, D = {v-d : d in Delta}, and assume d - rank(d) is even for every d in Delta (rank = number of smaller elements of Delta). If the long leg takes [1,B]\D in decreasing order, alternating, it visits each label of [1,B] exactly once except the k labels B-l-pi(d), where pi(d) = (d+rank(d))/2.
Proof: read w = B, B-1, ..., 1. Invariant when the tip is on a low turn at label lam (lam=0 is the centre) and u-1 is the largest label neither visited nor skipped: w = u-1-lam, all labels <= lam are visited, all labels >= u are visited or skipped. If w is not in D, the tip goes up to u-1; by the parity assumption w-1 is not in D either, and the tip comes down to lam+1, giving state (lam+1, u-1, w-2). If w is in D, label u-1 is skipped, giving state (lam, u-1, w-1). When w = v-d is skipped, lam = l + (d-rank(d))/2, so the skipped label is B-l-pi(d).

2. Short leg i covers labels B+i and 2l+i+delta_i. So the deal is valid iff, with c = B-3l, the map i -> c-i-delta_i is a bijection of {1..k} onto Pi = {pi(d)} (plus l >= 0 and v - max Delta >= 1, which hold for the designs below).

3. Designs.
- Odd k: Delta = Pi = [0,k-1]; pi_i = (k-1)/2+i for i <= (k-1)/2 and i-(k+1)/2 otherwise, a bijection.
- Even k=2h: Delta = [0,h-1] u [h+2,2h+1], Pi = [0,h-1] u [h+1,2h]; odd i = 2h-1-2s has pi = s, even i = 2j has pi = 2h+1-j.
- Stacking: if (c, delta) is a design for q with max Delta = q-1+2g and g <= q, then T copies with Delta-copy J at offset 3qJ and Pi-copy J at 2qJ, where i-block J uses Delta-copy T-1-J and Pi-copy J, form a design for Tq with c* = 3q(T-1)+c, because Jq + 3q(T-1-J) + 2qJ is constant and (3qJ+Jq)/2 = 2qJ.
- Three shifted copies of the odd design for q, with Delta-blocks at (0,q,4q) and Pi-blocks at (0,q,3q), or at (0,3q,4q) and (0,2q,3q), give c = 4q+(3q-1)/2 and 5q+(3q-1)/2; the plain odd design for 3q gives 3q+(3q-1)/2. With q prime to 3 these hit all residues, and stacking preserves the residue.

4. Limitation (proved). For any design, sum Pi = (sum Delta + k(k-1)/2)/2, so 4kc = k(3k+1) + 6 sum Delta and kc ≡ k (mod 3). If 3 does not divide k then c ≡ 1: this architecture cannot reach r+k ≡ 0 or 2 (mod 3).

NEGATIVE
1. The top-block rule cannot cover k not divisible by 3 with r+k ≡ 0 or 2 (mod 3) (proved by the sum argument). Smallest uncovered spiders with at least three legs: k=2 with r=1, 3, 4 (only r ≡ 2 mod 3, r >= 2 is covered). Valid deals for these exist by the README search, with other architectures.
2. k=1, legs (r,2): no valid deal exists at all, with any signs, for r = 4, 13, 14, 16, 19, 20, 23 (own brute force in k1tail.py; spiderzig FREE agrees on 13, 14, 16, 19, 20). So "all r" is false for k=1; this is a two-legged spider, outside Conjecture S's tested range of three or more legs.
3. Long leg zigzag with the short legs' numbers in at most 2 intervals (explore.py, r <= 40): for odd k only r+k ≡ 1 (mod 3) plus sporadic r; for even k almost nothing (k=2 only r=3; k=4 only r <= 6). With at most 3 intervals, k=5 still misses r = 15, 19, 25, 26, 28, 31, 32, 34 (r <= 36), smallest failure r=15.
4. Single-gap design Delta = [0,a-1] u [a+2,k+1] with a fixed greedy matching (even i ascending, then odd i, largest free delta): first gets stuck at k=63, then 102, 168, .... It is kept only as an option (GREEDY=1) and is not needed, since the tower design replaces it.
5. Product of a gapped design for 3 with the odd design for q does not change the residue when 3 divides q; this is why the tower strips all factors of 3 first.
6. Head and tail gadgets fail: skipping low labels at the start of the long leg cannot work because label 1 would need two adjacent top numbers, and a hole left at the centre for small numbers cannot be reached by a short leg.
7. Heuristic, not proved: for fixed k and large r the long leg must alternate except on O(k) numbers, so every architecture gives only residue classes of r. The two-block deals spiderzig finds for k=5, r=28 look like a "mod 7" type. Covering the remaining residues for k not divisible by 3 would need such further architectures; none was worked out.
