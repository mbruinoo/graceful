L-FAMILY: non-anchors of every degree.  Statements, complete arguments, machine checks.
=====================================================================================
Status words as in CLAUDE.md.  Nothing here proves the Graceful Tree Conjecture or Conjecture B.
"Proved" below means: a complete written argument is given in this file and every numbered step was also
checked by machine on all small cases (section 10).  It has NOT yet been read by independent referees.

Tree.  L(l; h1, h2): v has l leaf neighbours and one more neighbour c; c has two further neighbours
(the "hubs"); the hubs carry h1 and h2 leaves.  n = l+h1+h2+4 vertices, m = n-1 edges, deg(v) = l+1.
Labels 0..m (README: 1..n), v = 0.  By README Theorem 7 a growth placement anchored at v is a graceful
labelling in which edge lengths strictly decrease along every path away from v.

RESULTS
  Theorem L (lower bound; Proved).  Let p be even, q odd, p < q.  If l < p/2 then v is NOT an anchor of
      L(l; p, q).
  Theorem U (upper bound; Proved, explicit labelling).  Let p >= 2 be even and q >= 1 arbitrary.
      If l >= p/2 then v is an anchor of L(l; p, q).
  Corollary 1 (Proved).  For p even, q odd, p < q: v is an anchor of L(l; p, q) if and only if l >= p/2.
      (This is the earlier line's Observation "least l = h1/2", now for all even h1 < odd h2.)
  Corollary 2 (Proved).  For every l >= 1, in L(l; 2l+2, 2l+3) (5l+9 vertices) the vertex v has degree l+1
      and is not an anchor.  So non-anchors of every degree >= 2 exist; degree 4 at 24 vertices (L(3;8,9)).
  Main Theorem (Proved; no parity assumption).  Let 1 <= p < q and 2d < p.  In every solution of the reduced
      problem R(p,q,d) (section 1) the hub with q leaves has label 1, or the hubs have labels (P,Q) = (1,3).

1. Reduction to a problem on the number line 1..N  (Lemma R)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Reduced problem R(h1,h2,d).  N = h1+h2+2+d, a = N+1.  A SOLUTION consists of
  * two different hub labels g1, g2 in [1,N]; hub i has edge length x_i = a - g_i;
  * for each hub i a set W_i of h_i leaf lengths, all < x_i, each with a direction: a leaf of hub i with
    length w has label g_i + w ("up") or g_i - w ("down");
  * a set Gamma of d numbers ("gaps");
such that  {x1,x2}, W1, W2, Gamma partition [1,N]                                   (lengths)
and        {g1,g2}, the h1+h2 leaf labels, Gamma partition [1,N]                    (labels).
Write S = [1,N] minus Gamma.  So S is at the same time the set of all hub/leaf LABELS and the set of all
hub/leaf LENGTHS.  Picture: the number line 1..N; every non-gap point carries one vertex (a hub or a hub
leaf) and is also the length of exactly one hub edge or hub-leaf edge.

Lemma R.  If v is an anchor of L(l; h1, h2) then R(h1,h2,d) has a solution for some 0 <= d <= l.
Argument.  Take a growth placement with v = 0; root the tree at v.  Let a be the label of c; the edge vc has
length a.  A leaf at v with label w has length w.  By Theorem 7 the edge from c to hub i has length
x_i < a and every leaf edge at hub i has length < x_i.  Hence every length > a belongs to a leaf at v, and
since such a leaf has label = length, every label > a belongs to a leaf at v.  So both hubs and all hub
leaves have labels < a; in particular hub i sits at a - x_i (not a + x_i).  Let d be the number of leaves
at v with label < a; the other l - d leaves at v use exactly the numbers a+1..m.  Put N = a-1.  The numbers
1..N are, as labels: the two hubs, the h1+h2 hub leaves, d leaves at v; so N = h1+h2+2+d.  As lengths:
x1, x2, the hub-leaf lengths, and the lengths (= labels) of those d leaves at v.  Take Gamma = the labels of
those d leaves.  []
(Conversely a solution of R(h1,h2,d) gives a growth placement of L(d;h1,h2) anchored at v: v = 0, c = a,
gaps = leaves at v; lengths decrease away from v by construction.  Not needed below except in section 8.)

2. Notation
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fix a solution.  e < g are the two hub labels; E is the hub at e ("low hub"), G the hub at g.
X = a - e and Y = a - g are their edge lengths, so X > Y and X - Y = g - e.  hE, hG = numbers of leaves.
   A = up-lengths of E     (label e + w)        D = down-lengths of E   (label e - w)
   U = up-lengths of G     (label g + w)        V = down-lengths of G   (label g - w)
|A|+|D| = hE, |U|+|V| = hG.  For a set M of integers and integers r <= s write M[r,s] for the number of
elements of M in [r,s], and M(r,s] = M[r+1,s].  [statement] is 1 if the statement holds, else 0.

(B1) Every length in S is <= X (hub edges are X and Y < X; leaf lengths are smaller than their hub's edge).
     So the e-1 numbers X+1..N are gaps:   e - 1 <= d,   and every label is <= X.  Consequently
        A in [1, X-e],   U in [1, Y-e]  (g+u <= X),   D in [1, e-1],   V in [1, g-1] and V in [1, Y-1],
     and g <= X (g is a label), i.e. Y >= e.

3. The window identity  (STAR)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Lemma W.  For every integer t >= 0:
   A(t-e, t] + U(t-g, t]  =  [e<=t] + [g<=t] - [X<=t] - [Y<=t]  +  phiV(t)  +  phiD(t),              (STAR)
where  phiV(t) = (number of v in V with v >= g-t) - V[1,t]   and   phiD(t) = (number of w in D with
w >= e-t) - D[1,t].   phiV(t) = 0 for t >= g-1 and phiD(t) = 0 for t >= e-1.
Argument.  Count the points of S in [1,t] twice.  As labels: hub E if e <= t, hub G if g <= t, an up-leaf
of E with length w if w <= t-e, a down-leaf of E if e-w <= t, an up-leaf of G if w <= t-g, a down-leaf of
G if g-w <= t.  As lengths: X if X <= t, Y if Y <= t, and every leaf length <= t.  Both counts equal the
number of non-gap points in [1,t].  Subtract:  A[1,t] - A[1,t-e] = A(t-e,t], same for U, and the down-leaves
give phiV, phiD.  If t >= g-1 then all of V (which lies in [1,g-1]) is counted in both terms of phiV. []
Picture: slide a window of width e over the up-lengths of E and a window of width g over the up-lengths of
G, both ending at t.  The two windows together hold exactly 2 points while g <= t < Y, exactly 1 point
while max(g,Y) <= t < X, and none from X on.

Consequences used below.
(T1) Sum of labels = sum of lengths over S:
        e(1+hE) + g(1+hG) - 2 sum(D) - 2 sum(V) = X + Y = 2a - e - g,   a = hE+hG+3+d,   hence
        2d = (e-2)(hE+2) + (g-2)(hG+2) + 2 - 2 sum(V) - 2 sum(D).
(PAR) Parity.  From (T1), e(hE+2) + g(hG+2) is even.  If one hub has an even number p of leaves and the
      other an odd number q, then (label of the q-hub)*(q+2) is even, so THE q-HUB HAS AN EVEN LABEL.
(I)  Sum (STAR) over all t >= g.  A point a' of A lies in the window (t-e,t] for t = a'..a'+e-1; of these,
     min(e, g-a') are < g when a' < g.  A point u of U lies in (t-g,t] for t = u..u+g-1, of which g-u are
     < g when u < g.  The right side is 2 - [X<=t] - [Y<=t] for t >= g, with sum (X-g) + max(Y-g,0).  So
        e|A| + g|U| - Lam = X - g + max(Y-g, 0),
        Lam := sum over a' in A, a' < g of min(e, g-a')  +  sum over u in U, u < g of (g-u)   >= 0.
     If g <= Y, substituting X = hE+hG+3+d-e and Y = X-g+e:
        2d = (e-2)|A| + (g-2)|U| - 2|D| - 2|V| + e + 3g - 6 - Lam.                                    (I)
(B4) (STAR) at t = g-1 (note g-1 >= e):   A(g-1-e, g-1] + U[1,g-1] = 1 - [Y <= g-1].
     So at most one element of U is smaller than g; write iota = 1 if there is one, else 0.
     If Y < g then U has no element below g, and since U lies in [1,Y-1], U is empty.

4. Far case: g > Y  (Lemma F)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Assume g > Y.  By (B4) U is empty: all leaves of G point down.
Step F1.  For Y <= t <= g-1, (STAR) reads  A(t-e,t] = 1 + 0 - 0 - 1 + phiV(t) = -V[1, g-t-1]
          (V lies below Y <= t, so V[1,t] = |V|).  Both sides are therefore 0:
              V lies in [g-Y, Y-1]   and   A has no element in (Y-e, g-1].
Step F2.  For g <= t <= X-1, (STAR) reads A(t-e,t] = 1: every window of width e ending in [g, X-1] holds
          exactly one point of A.  If g < X: the window (g-e, g] has no point below g (F1), so g is in A.
          If g+ie is in A and g+ie < X, the windows ending at g+ie, ..., g+(i+1)e-1 (as far as they end
          before X) already contain g+ie, so A has no point strictly between g+ie and g+(i+1)e in that
          range, and if g+(i+1)e <= X-1 the window ending there forces g+(i+1)e into A.  The last point
          g+i*e reached is <= X-1 < g+(i*+1)e; but A lies in [1,X-e], so g+(i*+1)e <= X, hence = X.
          Therefore e divides X-g = Y-e;  write Y = y*e (y >= 1), and
              A intersected with [g, X-1]  =  { g, g+e, ..., X-e },   y-1 points.   (Also true if g = X.)
Step F3 (gaps).  Gaps are forced at: X+1..N (e-1 numbers); the numbers strictly between g and X that are
          not g+ie ((y-1)(e-1) numbers: they are not in A, exceed Y so are not leaf lengths of G, and
          exceed e); the c := g-Y-1 numbers strictly between Y and g (not in A by F1, above all G-lengths,
          above e-1 since Y >= e).  Let gam = number of gaps in [1,Y-1].  Then exactly
              d = y(e-1) + c + gam.
          Lengths in [1,Y-1] are V, D, the part of A below Y, and gam gaps, so with F2
              hE + hG = (Y - 1 - gam) + (y - 1) = Y + y - 2 - gam,     hG = |V| <= 2Y - g = Y - 1 - c.

Lemma F(a).  If g > Y and e >= 2 and the two hubs have different numbers of leaves p < q, then 2d > p.
   Argument.  p <= (hE+hG-1)/2 <= (ye + y - 3)/2 < y(e+1)/2 <= 2y(e-1) <= 2d, because e+1 <= 4(e-1). []

Lemma F(b).  If g > Y and e = 1 and hE >= 1, then hE >= hG.  (A low hub at 1 cannot have fewer leaves.)
   Argument.  Suppose 1 <= hE < hG =: q; then q >= 2.
   Here D is empty, y = Y, d = c + gam, hE = 2Y - 2 - gam - q.
   For 1 <= t <= Y-1 (STAR) reads  [t in A] = 1 + phiV(t) = 1 - (cV(t) - cB(t)),  where cV(t) = V[1,t] and
   cB(t) counts the labels B = { g - v : v in V } that are <= t.  So
        cV(t) - cB(t) is 0 or 1 for 1 <= t <= Y-1, and it is 1 whenever t is not in A (e.g. t in V).
   Sort V as v_1 < ... < v_q and B as b_1 < ... < b_q, b_i = g - v_{q+1-i}.  At t = v_i the difference is 1,
   so b_i > v_i.  At t = v_{i+1} the difference is 1, so b_i <= v_{i+1}  (i <= q-1).  Also b_q = g - v_1 <= Y
   by F1.  The points of [1,Y-1] outside A are exactly the disjoint intervals [v_i, b_i - 1]; removing the
   q points of V leaves the gaps:
        gam = sum_{i=1..q} (b_i - v_i - 1).
   For i <= q-1, b_i - v_i - 1 <= v_{i+1} - v_i - 1 = number of integers strictly between v_i and v_{i+1}.
   Let r = number of integers strictly between v_1 and v_q that are not in V.  Then the terms i = 1..q-1
   add up to at most r, and the term i = q equals the term i = 1 (both are g - v_1 - v_q - 1), which is
   <= r when q >= 2.  So  gam <= 2r.   Since V and those r integers lie in [g-Y, Y-1] = [c+1, Y-1]:
   q + r <= Y - 1 - c.   Now hE <= q - 1 says 2Y - 2 - gam - q <= q - 1, i.e.
        gam >= 2(Y - 1 - q) + 1 >= 2(r + c) + 1 > 2r >= gam,   a contradiction.   []

5. Near case g <= Y, low hub at e >= 2  (Lemma N2)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Assume g <= Y.  The sets  A below g,  U below g,  V,  D  are disjoint sets of lengths inside [1, g-1], so
        (number of points of A below g)  <=  g - 1 - iota - |V| - |D|,   and it is also <= |A|.
Every term of Lam coming from A is <= e and the single possible term from U is <= g-1.  Hence
   (alpha)  Lam <= e(g - 1 - iota - |V| - |D|) + (g-1) iota,
   (beta)   Lam <= e|A| + (g-1) iota.
Insert into (I):
   (alpha)  2d >= (e-2)(hE + |V|) + (g-2)|U| - (e-3)g + 2e - 6 - (g-1-e) iota,
   (beta)   2d >= -2 hE - 2|V| + (g-2)|U| + e + 3g - 6 - (g-1) iota.

Lemma N2.  Assume g <= Y.
   (i)  If e = 2 then 2d >= hG.
   (ii) If e >= 3 then 2d >= hE + hG + e - 3.
Argument.  (i) (alpha) with e = 2:  2d >= (g-2)|U| + (g-2) - (g-3) iota.  The labels g-v (v in V) are
   different points of [1,g-1] other than e, so |V| <= g-2.  If U is empty, iota = 0 and
   2d >= g-2 >= |V| = hG.  If |U| >= 1:  2d >= (g-2)|U| + 1 >= |U| + (g-2) >= hG, because
   (g-3)(|U|-1) >= 0  (g >= 3).
   (ii) Take (3/e) times (alpha) plus (1 - 3/e) times (beta)  (for e = 3 this is (alpha) itself).
   Coefficients:  hE: 3(e-2)/e - 2(e-3)/e = 1;  |V|: the same, 1;  |U|: g-2;  g: -3(e-3)/e + 3(e-3)/e = 0;
   constant: 3(2e-6)/e + (e-3)(e-6)/e = e-3;   iota: -3(g-1-e)/e - (e-3)(g-1)/e = -(g-4).   So
        2d >= hE + |V| + (g-2)|U| + e - 3 - (g-4) iota.
   If iota = 0: (g-2)|U| >= |U|.  If iota = 1 then |U| >= 1 and (g-2)|U| - (g-4) = (g-2)(|U|-1) + 2 >= |U|.
   Either way 2d >= hE + |V| + |U| + e - 3.  []

6. Near case g <= Y, low hub at 1  (Lemma N1)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Lemma N1.  Assume e = 1, g <= Y, and the low hub has fewer leaves:  p := hE < hG =: q, and p >= 1.
           Then g = 3, or 2d >= p.
Argument.  D is empty, |A| = p.  Write k = |U|, j = |V|, k + j = q.  The labels g - v lie in [2, g-1]
   (label 1 is the hub E), so V lies in [1, g-2] and j <= g-2.
   g = 2 is impossible:  V is empty and (I) gives 2d = -p + 1 - Lam, so p = 1, Lam = 0, d = 0; Lam = 0 means
   the length 1 is in neither A nor U; it is not X or Y (Y >= g = 2); so 1 is a gap, but d = 0.
   So let g >= 4 (g = 3 is the exception in the statement).
   k >= 1:  (STAR) at t = g.  If g < Y it reads [g in A] + U[1,g] = 2.  If g = Y it reads
   [g in A] + U[1,g] = 1 and g = Y is a hub edge, not in A.  Either way U has a point <= g.
   From (I) with e = 1:   2d = -p + (g-2)k - 2j + 3g - 5 - Lam,  and
        Lam <= (g - 1 - iota - j) + iota (g - u1)      (u1 = the element of U below g, if iota = 1),
   so   2d >= -p + (g-2)k - j + 2g - 4 - iota (g - u1 - 1).                                        (N)
   Suppose 2d <= p-1.  With p <= q-1 = k+j-1, (N) gives
        3j >= (g-4)k + 2g - 1 - iota (g - u1 - 1).                                                 (NN)
   * iota = 0:  3j >= (g-4) + 2g - 1 = 3g-5, so j >= g-1, contradicting j <= g-2.
   * iota = 1, k >= 2:  3j >= 3g - 8 + u1.  If u1 = g-1: 3j >= 4g-9 > 3(g-2) (as g >= 4), impossible.
     If u1 <= g-2: V and u1 are different points of [1,g-2], so j <= g-3 and 3g-9 >= 3g-8+u1, impossible.
   * iota = 1, k = 1:  U = {u}, u < g.  Two facts.
     (F-a) For g <= t < Y (STAR) reads [t in A] + [t < u+g] = 2, for Y <= t < X it reads
           [t in A] + [t < u+g] = 1.  So Y <= u+g, all of [g, Y-1] is in A, and all of [u+g, X-1] is in A:
                p >= (Y-g) + (X-u-g) = (Y-g) + (Y-1-u) >= g-1-u.
     (F-b) For 1 <= t <= g-1 (STAR) reads [t in A] + [u <= t] = 1 + phiV(t), i.e. with W = V + {u},
           cW(t) - cB(t) = 1 - [t in A] is 0 or 1 and is 1 at the points of W  (B = {g-v}, as in Lemma F).
           Sort W: w_1 < ... < w_{j+1}; sort V: v_1 < ... < v_j; b_i = g - v_{j+1-i}.  As in Lemma F:
           w_i < b_i and b_i <= w_{i+1} for i = 1..j.   Let u = w_r.
           If 2 <= r <= j:  w_1 = v_1 < b_1 = g - v_j, and b_j = g - v_1 <= w_{j+1} = v_j; these say
           v_1 + v_j < g and v_1 + v_j >= g.  Impossible.  So u is the smallest or the largest point of W.
           - u smallest and j >= 1:  V lies in [u+1, g-2], so j <= g-2-u; with (F-a):
             p >= g-1-u > j = q-1 >= p.  Impossible.
           - u largest (this includes j = 0):  w_i = v_i < b_i = g - v_{j+1-i} for all i, so
             v_i + v_{j+1-i} <= g-1 and 2 sum(V) <= j(g-1).  By (T1) with e = 1, hE = p, hG = j+1:
             2d = -p + (g-2)(j+3) - 2 sum(V) >= -p + 3g - 6 - j,  so
             2d - p >= 3g - 6 - j - 2p >= 3(g - 2 - j) >= 0     (p <= j, j <= g-2).
     In every sub-case the supposition 2d <= p-1 is contradicted (or the configuration cannot occur). []

7. Main Theorem, Theorem L, Corollaries
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Main Theorem.  Let 1 <= p < q, 2d < p.  In a solution of R(p,q,d) let P, Q be the hubs with p, q leaves.
   Then Q has label 1, or (label of P, label of Q) = (1, 3).
Argument.  Let e be the smaller hub label.
   e >= 2:  if g > Y, Lemma F(a) gives 2d > p.  If g <= Y, Lemma N2 gives 2d >= hG >= p (e = 2; hG is p
            or q) or 2d >= hE + hG >= p (e >= 3).  Both contradict 2d < p.
   e = 1 and the low hub is Q: this is the first alternative.
   e = 1 and the low hub is P (hE = p < q = hG):  g > Y is impossible by Lemma F(b).  If g <= Y, Lemma N1
            gives g = 3 (second alternative) or 2d >= p (contradiction).  []

Theorem L.  Let p be even, q odd, p < q, l < p/2.  Then v is not an anchor of L(l; p, q).
Argument.  If it were, Lemma R gives a solution of R(p,q,d) with d <= l, so 2d < p (hence p >= 2).  By the
   Main Theorem the label of Q is 1 or 3, which is odd.  By (PAR) the label of Q is even.  Contradiction. []

Corollary 2.  L(l; 2l+2, 2l+3):  p = 2l+2 is even, q = 2l+3 is odd, l < l+1 = p/2.  deg(v) = l+1.  []

Remarks.  (1) Both exceptional configurations of the Main Theorem occur (e.g. R(3,5,1) has solutions with
hub labels (1,3) and (5,1), (7,1); section 10), so parity is exactly what kills them in Theorem L.
(2) The only places where "p < q" is used are Lemmas F(a), F(b), N1 and the step hG >= p.
(3) Where do the gaps go, in words: a hub at label e >= 2 forces e-1 gaps above its own edge and then (via
the width-e window) about one gap per leaf pair; a hub at label 1 with the fewer leaves is squeezed between
"its up-leaves fill whole intervals" (F-a) and "the down-leaves of the other hub pair up to sums below g"
(F-b), and only g = 3 escapes.

8. Theorem U: l = p/2 leaves suffice (explicit labelling)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Theorem U.  Let p = 2s >= 2 and q >= 1.  Then v is an anchor of L(s; p, q), hence of L(l; p, q) for all
   l >= s (README Lemma 6: a leaf can be added at the vertex with the smallest label).
Labelling (m = 3s+q+3, a = m):   v = 0,  c = a,  hub Q = 2 (edge a-2),  hub P = 3 (edge a-3);
   leaves at v:   a-1, a-4, a-7, ..., a-3s+2                     (s leaves; label = length)
   leaves of Q:   lengths 3, 4, ..., q+2, all up: labels 5, ..., q+4
   leaves of P:   length 1 up (label 4), length 2 down (label 1), and for i = 1..s-1 the two lengths
                  a-3i-3, a-3i-2, both up (labels a-3i, a-3i+1).
Check.  Note a - 3s = q+3.
   Labels:  0 (v) | 1 (P, length 2 down), 2 (Q), 3 (P), 4 (P, length 1 up) | 5..q+4 (leaves of Q) |
   the block [q+5, a-1] = [a-3s+2, a-1]: its points a-3i-1 (i = 0..s-1) are the leaves of v and its points
   a-3i, a-3i+1 (i = 1..s-1) are P-leaves; these are all 3s-2 points of the block | a (c).
   Lengths: 1, 2 (P) | 3..q+2 (Q) | the block [q+3, a-4] = [a-3s, a-4]: its points a-3i-3, a-3i-2
   (i = 1..s-1) are the P-pairs and its points a-3i-1 (i = 1..s-1) are leaves of v; these are all 3s-3
   points of the block | a-3 (edge of P), a-2 (edge of Q), a-1 (leaf of v), a (edge vc).
   Each label 0..m and each length 1..m is used once.  Leaf lengths at P are <= a-5 < a-3 and at Q are
   <= q+2 < a-2; hub edges are < a.  By Theorem 7 this is a growth placement anchored at v.  []
(q + 2 < a - 2 needs q + 4 < 3s + q + 3, true for s >= 1.  For s = 1 there are no P-pairs.)

9. Worked instance: L(1; 4, 5), 14 vertices -- v is not an anchor
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
p = 4 (hub P), q = 5 (hub Q), l = 1.  Suppose a growth placement with v = 0 exists.
Step 1 (Lemma R).  c has label a = 13 (then the leaf of v has a label below 13: d = 1, N = 12) or a = 12
   (the leaf of v is at 13: d = 0, N = 11).  Everything else lives on the line 1..N, a = N+1.
Step 2 (PAR).  Sum of labels = sum of lengths gives 6*gP + 7*gQ = 2a + 2(down lengths): gQ is even.
Step 3.  The numbers above the longer hub edge X = a - e are gaps, so e - 1 <= d <= 1: the low hub is at 1 or 2.
Step 4 (low hub at 2; then d = 1, N = 12, a = 13, X = 11, the one gap is 12, every number 1..11 is used).
   Length 10 cannot be a leaf of E (up: label 12 is the gap; down needs length < 2), and a leaf of G needs
   10 < Y <= 10.  So 10 = Y, G is at 3.  Length 9: not a leaf of G (label 12 or negative), so E-up, label 11.
