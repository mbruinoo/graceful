Tree.  L(l; h1, h2): v has l leaf neighbours and one more neighbour c; c has two more neighbours g1, g2
carrying h1, h2 leaves.  n = l+h1+h2+4, deg(v) = l+1.  "v is an anchor" = some growth placement has v at the
smallest label.

SUMMARY
(1) Lower bound: PROVED for the whole family, not only l = 1.
      Theorem L.  p even, q odd, p < q, l < p/2  ==>  v is not an anchor of L(l; p, q).
    Together with an explicit labelling for l = p/2 (Theorem U, proved, valid for every q >= 1) this gives
      Corollary 1.  For p even, q odd, p < q: v is an anchor of L(l;p,q)  <=>  l >= p/2.
      Corollary 2.  L(l; 2l+2, 2l+3) (5l+9 vertices) has the non-anchor v of degree l+1, for EVERY l >= 1.
    So non-anchors of unbounded degree exist, as a theorem.  The earlier Observation "least l = h1/2"
    (checked h2 <= 21, l <= 8) is now a statement for all even h1 < odd h2.
    The smallest instance L(1;4,5) (n = 14) has a short self-contained argument (PROOFS.txt section 9).
    Behind Theorem L is a parity-free statement (Main Theorem): if 1 <= p < q and fewer than p/2 leaves of v
    lie below c, then the q-hub has label 1 or the hubs are at (P,Q) = (1,3); parity (the q-hub must have
    an even label when p is even and q odd) then leaves nothing.
(2) Smallest tree with a non-anchor of degree >= 4: EXACTLY 24 vertices, and it is UNIQUE.
    All trees on n = 20, 21, 22, 23 vertices were searched (823,065 / 2,144,505 / 5,623,756 / 14,828,074
    trees; every vertex of degree >= 4, one per automorphism orbit): all are anchors, each with a labelling
    re-checked from the definition.  At n = 24 (39,299,897 trees) the complete list of non-anchors of degree
    >= 4 was determined (section B5): there is exactly one, the vertex v of L(3;8,9).  That it is a
    non-anchor is proved (Theorem L) and confirmed by three programs (DFS ascan2, DFS anchq, SAT).
    By-products: the complete lists of degree-2 non-anchors on 22 vertices (89 kinds) and of degree-3
    non-anchors on 23 vertices (2 kinds: L(2;8,9) and L(2;6,11), both covered by Theorem L).
(3) Every tree on n <= 23 vertices has an anchor of maximum degree (Observation, exhaustive).

A1. Statements and status
  Lemma R (Proved).  If v is an anchor of L(l;h1,h2), then for some 0 <= d <= l the following "reduced
     problem" R(h1,h2,d) has a solution: N = h1+h2+2+d, a = N+1; hubs at different labels g1,g2 in [1,N]
     with edge lengths a-g1, a-g2; hub i has h_i leaves, a leaf of length w (w < a-g_i) sits at g_i+w or
     g_i-w; d numbers are gaps (leaves of v: label = length); every number of [1,N] is used exactly once as
     a label and exactly once as a length.   (d = number of leaves of v with label below c's label a.)
  Lemma W, window identity (Proved).  With e < g the hub labels, X = a-e > Y = a-g their edges, A/D the
     up/down lengths at the low hub E and U/V at the other hub G, for every t >= 0:
        #A in (t-e,t] + #U in (t-g,t] = [e<=t]+[g<=t]-[X<=t]-[Y<=t] + (#V >= g-t) - (#V <= t)
                                         + (#D >= e-t) - (#D <= t).
     (Count the non-gap points of [1,t] once as labels and once as lengths.)  On the number line: a window
     of width e over E's up-lengths and a window of width g over G's up-lengths, both ending at t, hold
     together exactly 2 points for g <= t < Y and exactly 1 point for max(g,Y) <= t < X.
  Consequences (Proved): the e-1 numbers above X are gaps; parity (PAR): if p is even and q odd, the q-hub
     has an even label; identity (I): for g <= Y,
        2d = (e-2)|A| + (g-2)|U| - 2|D| - 2|V| + e + 3g - 6 - Lam,
     Lam = sum_{a' in A, a'<g} min(e, g-a') + sum_{u in U, u<g} (g-u) >= 0; at most one u in U is < g.
  Lemma F (far case g > Y; Proved).  Then G's leaves all point down, e divides Y, A above g is
     {g, g+e, .., X-e}, and d = (Y/e)(e-1) + (g-Y-1) + (gaps below Y).  (a) e >= 2, p < q ==> 2d > p.
     (b) e = 1 and 1 <= hE < hG is impossible.
  Lemma N2 (near case g <= Y, e >= 2; Proved).  e = 2 ==> 2d >= hG.   e >= 3 ==> 2d >= hE + hG + e - 3.
  Lemma N1 (near case g <= Y, e = 1, 1 <= hE = p < hG; Proved).  g = 3 or 2d >= p.
  Main Theorem (Proved).  1 <= p < q, 2d < p, solution of R(p,q,d)  ==>  label(Q) = 1 or (P,Q) = (1,3).
  Theorem L (Proved).  p even, q odd, p < q, l < p/2 ==> v is not an anchor of L(l;p,q).
  Theorem U (Proved).  p = 2s >= 2, q >= 1 arbitrary: v is an anchor of L(l;p,q) for all l >= s.
     Labelling for l = s (m = 3s+q+3): v = 0, c = m, Q-hub = 2, P-hub = 3; leaves of v at m-1, m-4, ..,
     m-3s+2; Q-leaves at 5..q+4 (lengths 3..q+2); P-leaves at 4 (length 1), 1 (length 2) and at m-3i,
     m-3i+1 (lengths m-3i-3, m-3i-2), i = 1..s-1.
  Corollaries 1, 2 (Proved) as in the summary.
  What is NOT covered: odd h1 (the least-l table there is irregular: values 0,1,2, earlier line's
     delta_table_17.txt); even p >= q; both even.  For those the Main Theorem still restricts solutions with
     2d < p (when p < q) to the two exceptional hub positions, but they can occur.

A3. Proof idea in pictures (full text: PROOFS.txt sections 1-9)
  Put c at the top.  Everything except v's leaves above c lives on the line 1..N; a leaf of v is a point that
  is "its own length" (a gap for the hubs).  Each hub is a point; an up-leaf is an arrow to the right by its
  length, a down-leaf an arrow to the left.  Every non-gap point is the head of one arrow (or a hub) and the
  length of one arrow (or of a hub edge).  Counting heads and lengths in [1,t] gives the window identity.
  * A hub at label e leaves e-1 dead points above its edge, and its up-arrows have a rigid period-e
    structure; summing the window identity shows 2d >= (number of leaves of the other hub) for e = 2 and
    2d >= all leaves for e >= 3.
  * A hub at 1 with the fewer leaves p: its up-arrows are unit steps, so they fill whole intervals; the
    other hub's down-arrows must interleave with their own heads (v_i + v_{j+1-i} just below g), which
    caps the down-lengths; the count (I) then forces 2d >= p unless the other hub is at 3.
  * Sum of labels = sum of lengths puts the hub with an odd number q of leaves at an even label when p is
    even.  So it is not at 1 or 3.

B5. The complete list at n = 24
  Reduction (uses only README Lemma 6: a leaf added at an anchor leaves it an anchor; equivalently, if v is a
  non-anchor of T and u is a leaf neighbour of v, then v is a non-anchor of T - u).
  Let v be a non-anchor of degree >= 4 in a 24-vertex tree T.
   (a) v has no leaf neighbour, or
   (b) v has a leaf neighbour u.  Then v is a non-anchor of T - u (23 vertices) of degree deg(v) - 1 >= 3;
       by B1 it is not >= 4, so deg(v) = 4 and v is a degree-3 non-anchor on 23 vertices.  Such a vertex
       either has no leaf neighbour (b1) or comes in the same way from a degree-2 non-anchor on 22 vertices

C. MAXIMUM-DEGREE ANCHORS
=============================================================================================================
Observation (exhaustive, n = 15..23 here; n <= 19 also by the earlier line): every tree has at least one
anchor of maximum degree.  For trees of maximum degree >= 4 this is part of section B (all their degree >= 4
vertices are anchors).  For trees of maximum degree 2 or 3 the same runs test the maximum-degree vertices in
turn until an anchor is found: "no max-deg anchor" = 0 and undecided = 0 in every part (table in B2).
At n = 24: every tree of maximum degree >= 4 has a maximum-degree anchor (by B5 the only non-anchor of degree
>= 4 is v in L(3;8,9), and there the maximum degree is 10).  Trees of maximum degree 2 or 3 on 24 vertices
were not examined, so the Observation is stated for n <= 23 only.
By Corollary 2 the family L(l;2l+2,2l+3) has v of degree l+1 < 2l+4 = maximum degree, so the L family never
makes v a maximum-degree vertex and says nothing against the Observation.
Status: Observation (all trees on n <= 23 vertices).  No argument.

=============================================================================================================
D. DEAD ENDS AND THINGS NOT DONE
=============================================================================================================
1. A direct scan of all degree >= 4 vertices at n = 24 with the DFS was estimated at 18 CPU-hours and not
   run; the leaf reduction of B5 replaced it.  The first attempt at B5 with the DFS alone (cap 2*10^9)
   stalled on undecided vertices (two of them took about a quarter of an hour each without an answer); it was stopped and
   re-run with a small cap plus SAT.  Lesson: the longest-first DFS is a poor decision procedure for hard
   anchors; SAT decided all 5,894 leftover vertices in under a second each.
2. Before the window identity was found, a top-down cascade ("which length can still reach label N, N-1, ...")
   was tried.  It settles the sub-case "all gaps above the longer hub edge" quickly (PROOFS.txt section 9,
   step 4) but branches without control once gaps are allowed inside; abandoned.
3. Parity alone (labels vs lengths mod 2) is not enough: it gives (PAR) and "a hub with an even number of
   leaves at an odd label has as many even as odd leaf lengths", which does not bound d.  Roots of unity of
   higher order were considered and dropped (down-leaves break the product form).
4. A first attempt at the low-hub-at-1 case through the gap sizes of the up-lengths (s_i = g-1-(u_{i+1}-u_i))
   gave d = sum s_i + sum (g-1-2v) but left one configuration open (one up-length below g); it was replaced
   by Lemma N1's interleaving argument.
5. The convex combination 3/e, 1-3/e in Lemma N2(ii) was found by elimination, not by a picture; a drawn
   argument for "low hub at e >= 3 needs 2d >= all leaves" would be better.
6. Odd h1: no rule.  The Main Theorem leaves the configurations "Q at 1" and "(P,Q) = (1,3)"; they are what
   makes the least-l table irregular for odd h1 and were not analysed.
7. Three hubs under c (L(l;h1,h2,h3)) and deeper trees: not touched.  The window identity extends verbatim
   (one window per hub), the case analysis does not.
8. Novelty was not checked against the literature (0-rotatable / 0-centred graceful labellings: Van Bussel
   2004, Anick 2016, Luiz-Campos-Richter 2020 treat label 0 without the growth condition).
9. Not done: maximum-degree anchors at n = 24 for trees of maximum degree <= 3; the smallest non-anchor of
   degree >= 5 (Corollary 2 gives one on 29 vertices, L(4;10,11); B5 shows none on n <= 24); a second
   implementation of the n = 20..23 enumeration.
10. check_main.py 22 35 was stopped early in the first session (complete for p <= 17) and not resumed.
