LINE "comprules" -- the composition rules of results/composition/REPORT.md, restated for referees
==========================================================================================

The Graceful Tree Conjecture is open and nothing here changes that.  Conjecture B of the README (every
tree has a growth placement) is open too.  This file does three things:

  1. restates Rules T, P, N, B, D, Propositions 1 and 2, the failing rules F1-F3 and the closure
     Observation of results/composition/REPORT.md ("the source") as self-contained claims, each with
     its full proof.  The proofs are the source's; where I changed wording or filled a step I say so
     in a line marked [changed] or [added].
  2. reports an independent brute-force check of every one of them.  The programs in this directory
     were written from README sections 1-3 and from the rule statements; they share no code with
     results/composition/.  (I compiled and ran the source's closure.cpp once, for one comparison,
     see 5.3.)
  3. answers the question "does any combination of these rules give a labelling recipe for a new
     infinite family, e.g. trees made of equal branches and nothing else?"   Answer: NO, not that I
     could find; section 6 says exactly what was tried, what is proved about why it fails, and what
     the rules do give.

Status words are those of CLAUDE.md.  "Proved" below means: a complete argument is written in this
file.  NO referee has read it yet, so by the repository rule these are "proved, awaiting the two
referees".  Novelty against the literature was not checked by me.

ONE THING I COULD NOT CONFIRM (details in 5.3).  The source's table says that Rules C, T, D reach
all 106 trees at n = 10 and that their smallest unreached trees have 13 vertices.  My two independent
engines, AND the source's own closure.cpp, reach 105 of 106 at n = 10: the tree "centre with two legs
of length 2 and one more neighbour that carries two legs of length 2" is not reached.  Every other
number of the source that I recomputed agrees exactly.

Everything else was confirmed.  Two things go beyond the source:
  * Theorem G (section 5.3): the closure of a set of placements under Rule T is the set of all paths
    of a small graph (the "tape graph").  This makes the closure a 20-second computation at n = 16
    and extends the closure Observation from n <= 16 to n <= 19 (C, T, B) and from n <= 14 to
    n <= 16 (C, T, Lemma 8).
  * Lemma B1 (converse of Rule B for t = 2), Lemma N1 and Lemma S (small proved obstructions used
    in section 6).


0. CONVENTIONS
--------------
Labels are 0..m, m = n-1 (README labels are these plus 1).  The bracket of length k is the edge
{p_k, p_k + k}, 0 <= p_k <= m-k.  A placement has one bracket of each length 1..m.  It is a GROWTH
placement if, taking brackets longest first, every bracket after the first has exactly one foot on
a label already used (README section 2).  The first bracket is always {0, m}.  By README Theorem 7
this is the same as: rooting the tree at the vertex labelled 0, edge lengths strictly decrease along
every path away from the root.

For a growth placement f on n vertices and 0 <= k <= m:
  time k        the moment when exactly the brackets longer than k are placed;
  Free_k(f)     the k labels still unused at time k (never 0 or m when k <= m-1);
  prefix        of f at time k: its brackets longer than k;   completion: its brackets of length <= k;
  l(v)          for a label v != 0: the length of the bracket that added v (so l(m) = m).
"Rule C" is the complement p_k -> m-k-p_k (labels i -> m-i); it maps growth placements to growth
placements because the definition is symmetric (README, end of section 1; Classical).

The picture for all of it: draw the labels 0..m as a row of cells (the tape), filled when used.
Whether a bracket may be placed depends only on which cells are filled, not on how they were joined.


1. RULE T (completion transplant)                                              STATUS: Proved
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Statement.  Let f and h be growth placements, with m_f and m_h edges, and 1 <= k < min(m_f, m_h).
Call a label a of h TOUCHED if it is used at time k (in h) and is a foot of a bracket of h of
length <= k.  Suppose there is an integer s with
   (1)  Free_k(f) = Free_k(h) + s, and
   (2)  for every touched label a of h, a + s is a label of f (0 <= a+s <= m_f) used at time k in f.
Then the prefix of f at time k, followed by the completion of h shifted by s (bracket i <= k goes to
{p_i(h)+s, p_i(h)+s+i}), is a growth placement on m_f + 1 vertices.  It draws the tree of the prefix
of f with the forest drawn by the completion of h attached, each touched label a identified with the
vertex of f labelled a+s.

      f :  X X . . . . X X X      prefix of f kept            (X used at time k, . free)
      h :    X . . . . X X X X    completion of h moved by s = -1; it touches the cells
                                  next to the run

Proof (source's, wording tightened).  The new placement has one bracket of each length 1..m_f.  Its
brackets longer than k are those of f, so at time k its used set is that of f.
Claim: for i = k, k-1, .., 0, at time i a label x in Free_k(f) is used in the new placement iff
x - s is used in h at time i.  This holds at i = k (neither is used).  Take the bracket of length
i <= k of h, with feet a (used in h at time i) and b (unused in h at time i).  Then b is in
Free_k(h), so b+s is in Free_k(f) and is unused by the claim.  For a there are two cases.  If a is
in Free_k(h), then a+s is in Free_k(f) and is used by the claim.  Otherwise a was already used at
time k, so a is touched, and a+s is a label of f used at time k by (2); it stays used.  So the
shifted bracket lies inside 0..m_f and has exactly one used foot, and it fills the cell b+s: the
claim holds at time i-1.  At time 0 every bracket has been placed with exactly one used foot.  The
tree statement is read off the labels.  QED
[changed: "k >= 1" made explicit; the sentence "lies inside 0..m_f" added; nothing else.]

Remark T1 (Proved; [added], it is what the programs use).  Given (1), hypothesis (2) is equivalent to
     (2')  every bracket of h of length <= k, shifted by s, lies inside 0..m_f.
Proof.  A foot of such a bracket is either in Free_k(h) -- then its shift is in Free_k(f), inside
1..m_f - 1 -- or it is touched.  So (2') says exactly that a+s is in 0..m_f for every touched a.  And
a touched label is not in Free_k(h), so a+s is not in Free_k(f) = Free_k(h)+s: if a+s is a label of f
at all, it is used at time k.  QED
So the source's sentence "hypothesis 2 is needed: if a touched label lands on an unused or missing
cell ..." can be sharpened: under (1) a touched label can never land on an unused cell, only outside
the tape, and then the result is not a placement on 0..m_f at all.  Under (1): product is a growth
placement  <=>  (2).

Check (check_TPN.py; every pair f, h of growth placements with 2..6 edges, every k, the s forced by
(1)):  86,420 applications with (1) and (2), all growth placements; 9,888 with (1) but not (2), none
a growth placement.  Both numbers equal the source's.  Extended to 2..7 edges (8 vertices):
1,150,684 and 159,096, same outcome.

What it gives.  No family by itself: it needs two placements whose tapes agree.


2. RULE P (run filling)                                                        STATUS: Proved
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Statement.  Let f be a growth placement on n vertices and 1 <= k <= m-1 such that Free_k(f) is one
run [x, x+k-1].  Let g be any growth placement on k+1 vertices (labels 0..k).  Then the prefix of f
at time k followed by g placed on [x-1, x+k-1] (bracket i of g goes to p_i(g) + x - 1), or on
[x, x+k] (to p_i(g) + x), is a growth placement.  Its tree is the prefix tree R of f with the tree
of g attached: the vertex of R labelled x-1 is identified with the vertex of g labelled 0
(respectively: the vertex labelled x+k with the vertex of g labelled k).

Proof (source's; second position spelled out).  0 and m are used from the start, so 1 <= x and
x+k-1 <= m-1; x-1 and x+k are labels of f, not in the run, hence used at time k.  First position:
the top bracket of g lands on {x-1, x+k-1}: one foot used, one not.  From then on, inside
[x-1, x+k-1] the used cells are exactly those of g's own build after its top bracket (both ends
used, the rest free), and g's brackets do not leave that interval; so each has exactly one used
foot.  Second position: the top bracket lands on {x, x+k}, x free and x+k used, and the same
argument applies (or apply Rule C to f and g).  Lengths: g uses 1..k, the prefix k+1..m.  QED
[changed: the second position is argued, the source only states it.]

Special cases (source's remarks, checked by reading):
  * k = m-1: the prefix is the top bracket; this is README Lemma 6.
  * README section 7 ("gluing at the top"): given O as there, raise its high labels and fill the gap
    with any placement; that is an f whose prefix is O and whose free set is one run.  So P contains
    the gluing statement.  [changed: the source says "f a staircase: this is the gluing statement";
    more exactly, the gluing statement is P for prefixes all of whose brackets jump over the run,
    and P also allows prefixes with brackets that lie on one side of the run.]

Check (check_TPN.py): 12,860 applications (f with <= 6 edges, every k with one run, every g, both
positions), all growth placements, tree statement correct in all (source: 12,860).  With <= 7
edges: 146,756, all correct.


3. RULE N (nesting at a clean cut)                                             STATUS: Proved
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Statement.  Let f be a growth placement of T on n vertices and 1 <= L <= m a cut (between labels
L-1 and L) such that the brackets jumping over it (one foot <= L-1, one foot >= L) are exactly the
c longest.  Put j = n - c; the brackets that do not jump are those of lengths 1..j-1.  Suppose
u = L - j >= 0 and the label u is 0 or was added by a jumping bracket (l(u) >= j).  Let h be a
growth placement on g + j vertices, g >= 1, in which the labels 1..j-1 are the last j-1 to be added
(no condition if j = 1), and let U' be the tree drawn by its brackets of length >= j: its root has
label 0 and its other g vertices have the labels j..j+g-1.  Then the following is a growth placement
on n + g vertices of T with U' glued by its root at the vertex labelled u:
   * labels of f that are >= L move up by g (a gap [L, L+g-1] opens);
   * the g brackets of U' are placed with label 0 -> u and label y -> u + y.
(The mirror statement follows by Rule C.)

Proof (source's, with two sentences added).  U' is connected and contains 0 because the brackets of
length >= j are a prefix of h; it has g brackets, and its labels other than 0 are those not among
1..j-1, i.e. j..j+g-1.  [added]
Lengths: the jumping brackets grow by g and become j+g..m+g; U' uses j..j+g-1; the others keep
1..j-1.  Labels: U' occupies u and u + [j, j+g-1] = [L, L+g-1], the gap; u <= L-1 is not moved.
Order (longest first): first the c jumping brackets; they are the c longest of f, so they are f's
prefix, placed in the same order on the relabelled cells, with the same used/unused pattern.  Now u
is used (it is 0 or was added by a jumping bracket).  Next the brackets of U': its top bracket is
{u, L+g-1}, one used foot; the gap cells are not labels of f, so inside {u} + gap the build is that
of h.  Finally the non-jumping brackets of f: each lies on one side of the cut, its feet are labels
of f, and the cells of f are used or not exactly as in f at that moment (U' filled only gap cells).
QED

The condition on u is necessary: otherwise the top bracket of U' has no used foot (u is a label of f
that is not yet used when the jumping brackets are done).

Special cases (source's): g = 1 is README Lemma 8 (U' an edge).  j = 1: all brackets jump, f is a
staircase (README Theorem 9) and the statement is Rule P.  Every cut of a staircase is clean (its
brackets are nested intervals, so the ones containing a cut are the longest).

Lemma N1 (Proved; [added]).  In Rule N, if g <= j then U' is a star centred at its root.  So Rule N
inserts a piece that is not a bunch of leaves only when the piece has more edges than the number of
non-jumping brackets plus one.
Proof.  Root U' at label 0.  A child of the root has label = its edge length x in [j, j+g-1].  A
vertex at depth 2 would have label x + y or x - y with y != x another length in [j, j+g-1].  But
x + y >= 2j + 1 > j + g - 1 and |x - y| <= g - 1 < j when g <= j, so neither is a label of U'.  QED

Check (check_TPN.py): 27,871 applications (T, T' on <= 7 vertices, every clean cut, every admissible
h), all growth placements, tree statement correct; 1,234 with u >= 0 but the condition on u violated,
none a growth placement (source: 27,871 and 1,234).  On <= 8 vertices: 252,187 and 10,506, same
outcome.  check_misc.py: j = 1 only for staircases (127 cases), every cut of every staircase with
<= 7 edges is clean, and Lemma N1 on all h with <= 8 edges (4,091 truncations with g <= j, all stars).

What it adds (Observation, n <= 14; source's, confirmed): started from staircases, C + N derives
exactly the same placements as C + Lemma 8: 66, 178, 502, 1408, 4064, 11600, 33644, 96768 placements
and 10, 21, 43, 93, 205, 474, 1112, 2663 trees for n = 7..14 (logs/C_N.log, logs/C_L8.log; here h
ranges over derived placements).


4. RULE B (block designs) AND RULE D (mirror windows)                          STATUS: Proved
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Setting.  Let f be a growth placement of T on p vertices and t >= 2.  Multiply every label by t.
The labels in 0..(p-1)t that are not multiples of t fall into BLOCKS j = 0..p-2, block j being
jt+1, .., jt+t-1 (between the vertices labelled j and j+1).  The lengths that are not multiples of
t fall into BANDS b = 0..p-2, band b being bt+1, .., bt+t-1.
A DESIGN gives each block j an owner v(j) in {0..p-1}.  Its band is
        beta(j) = j - v(j)       if v(j) <= j,          beta(j) = v(j) - j - 1    if v(j) > j
(the number of labels of T strictly between the owner and the block).  The design is VALID if
   (1) beta is a bijection from the blocks onto {0..p-2}, and
   (2) for every block, beta(j) < l(v(j))   (no condition when v(j) = 0).

Rule B.  If the design is valid then for every t >= 2 the tree T with (t-1) * #{blocks owned by v}
new leaves on each vertex v has a growth placement on (p-1)t + 1 vertices: the vertex labelled i
gets label ti, and the leaves of the owner of block j get the labels of block j.

      t = 3, T = star 0-2 0-1, block 0 owned by label 2, block 1 owned by label 1
      label   0   1   2   3   4   5   6
              X   .   .   X   .   .   X        X = T times 3: brackets 0-6 and 0-3
                 block 0     block 1           block 0 hangs on 6: lengths 5, 4 (band 1)
                                               block 1 hangs on 3: lengths 2, 1 (band 0)
      placement, longest first:  0-6 1-6 2-6 0-3 3-5 3-4

Proof (source's; the sentences on labels and on the owner 0 are added).  Labels: T takes the
multiples of t, and every other label lies in exactly one block, which has one owner.  Lengths: a
leaf at jt+d (1 <= d <= t-1) on the owner tv has length (j-v)t + d = beta*t + d if v <= j, and
(v-j)t - d = beta*t + (t-d) if v > j; as d runs over 1..t-1 these are the t-1 lengths of band beta.
By (1) the leaf lengths are all the non-multiples of t below (p-1)t, once each, and the brackets of
T are the multiples.  Order: a bracket of length t*i joins two multiples of t, and the multiples of
t are filled only by such brackets, so these behave as in f.  A leaf bracket of band beta needs its
owner used and its leaf unused.  The leaf label is the foot of no other bracket.  The owner 0 is
used from the first bracket on.  An owner v != 0 was added by the bracket of length t*l(v), which
is longer than every length of band beta (at most beta*t + t - 1) iff l(v) >= beta + 1.  QED

Lemma B1 (Proved; [added] -- the source states the "only if" half as a computed fact).  Let M be
even.  A growth placement on M+1 vertices in which every odd label is a leaf is exactly a Rule B
product with t = 2 of a growth placement on M/2 + 1 vertices and a valid design; and the design is
determined by it.  Consequently:  if T with lambda(v) new leaves on v has a growth placement in
which T sits on the even labels, then T with (t-1)*lambda(v) new leaves on v has a growth placement
for every t >= 2.
Proof.  Let every odd label be a leaf.  An edge at an odd label is its only edge, so no edge joins
two odd labels; the M/2 edges at odd labels have odd lengths, hence are all the odd lengths, and
the other M/2 edges join even labels and have the even lengths.  Removing leaves keeps a tree, so
the even labels carry a tree T; a bracket between even labels has exactly one used foot when it is
placed and even cells are filled only by such brackets, so halving gives a growth placement f of T.
Let the leaf 2j+1 hang on 2v.  Its length is 2*beta + 1 with beta as in the definition; lengths are
distinct, so (1) holds; the leaf bracket needs 2v used, i.e. v = 0 or 2*beta + 1 < 2*l(v), i.e.
beta < l(v): that is (2).  The converse is Rule B.  QED

Remarks (source's, checked).
  2. The band-0 leaves are the last t-1 brackets and fill one block, a run with a vertex of T on
     each side.  By Rule P that bunch of t-1 leaves can be replaced by any growth placement on t
     vertices, glued by its label 0 at the left neighbour or by its label t-1 at the right one.
  3. Leaves on the vertex labelled 0 can be added afterwards (README Lemma 6).
  4. Placements that use the residues in other ways exist (S(2,2,2) = 0-6 0-5 1-5 0-3 4-6 2-3 is the
     star 0-2 0-1 times 3 with a path through two blocks; verified growth); no rule was found.

Rule D.  Let f be a growth placement of T on p vertices and 1 <= w <= p.  Suppose for every label
1 <= i <= w-1:     l(i) >= w - 2i  if 2i <= w-1,      l(i) >= 2i - w + 1  if 2i >= w.
Then for every t >= 2 the tree T with t-1 leaves on each vertex labelled 1..w-1 and (p-w)(t-1)
leaves on the vertex labelled 0 has a growth placement: the leaves of the vertex labelled i take
block w-1-i, the vertex labelled 0 takes the blocks >= w-1.
Proof (source's).  This is the design v(j) = w-1-j for j < w-1, v(j) = 0 for j >= w-1.  The bands
of the root's blocks are j >= w-1.  For 1 <= i <= w-1 the block is j = w-1-i and the band is
w-1-2i if 2i <= w-1 (then i <= j) and 2i-w if 2i >= w (then i > j): the numbers below w-1 of the
parity of w-1, respectively of w, each once.  So (1) holds, and (2) is the hypothesis.  QED
For w = p, with t-1 more leaves on the root by Lemma 6, this is the labelling of "T Delta K_{1,t-1}"
of Stanton-Zarnke / Koh-Rogers-Tan (Classical); see F3.

F3 and Proposition 2.  Take growth placements f of T (p vertices) and g of T' (q vertices), v' the
vertex of T' labelled 0.  Blocks here are [iq, iq+q-1], i = 0..p-1.  Put the vertex x of copy i at
label iq + g(x) if x is at even distance from v', and at (p-1-i)q + g(x) otherwise; join the copies
at v' along T (copy i's v' to copy i''s v' for every edge {i, i'} of f).
  * Observation: the result is a graceful tree for all 361 pairs of placements with 2 <= p, q <= 5
    (the construction is Classical: Stanton-Zarnke, Koh-Rogers-Tan).
  * It is a growth placement in 28 of the 361, and in each of those T' is a star centred at v'.
    Smallest failure: T = K_2, T' = path on 3 vertices rooted at an end: 0-5 1-5 0-3 2-4 2-3, and the
    bracket 2-4 has no used foot.
  * For the 76 pairs in which T' is a star centred at v', the labelling is a growth placement
    exactly when f satisfies the hypothesis of Rule D with w = p (my addition; 76 of 76 agree).
Proposition 2 (STATUS: Proved).  For p >= 2 this labelling is a growth placement only if T' is a
star centred at v'.
Proof (source's).  For an edge e of T' let s(e) = g(end at odd distance from v') - g(other end);
|s(e)| <= q-1.  In copy 0 the edge e joins 0*q + g(even end) to (p-1)q + g(odd end): length
(p-1)q + s(e).  In copy p-1 it has length (p-1)q - s(e).  The vertex labelled 0 is v' of copy 0, and
the path from it to any vertex of copy 0 or of copy p-1 enters that copy at its v'; by README
Theorem 7 lengths must decrease along paths leaving v' in both copies.  For two consecutive edges
e_1, e_2 on a path leaving v' that needs s(e_1) > s(e_2) and -s(e_1) > -s(e_2).  So T' has no vertex
at distance 2 from v'.  QED
Check: check_BD.py, numbers above; equal to the source's (361, 28, the smallest failure).

5.2  Proposition 1 (STATUS: Proved).  If f, g, h are staircases, then the complement of f, every
Rule P product of f with g, and every Rule T product of f with h are staircases.  Hence Rules C, P,
T, started from staircases, derive only staircases, i.e. only caterpillars (README Theorem 2).
Proof (source's, expanded).  A staircase is p_m = 0, p_k - p_{k+1} in {0, 1}.  C: the difference
becomes 1 - (p_k - p_{k+1}).  At time k a staircase f has used cells [0, a] + [b, m], its bracket
k+1 is {a, b} and the free set is the run (a, b).  P: the top bracket of g goes to {a, b-1} or
{a+1, b}, which is a staircase step from {a, b}, and g continues as a staircase.  T: at time k the
staircase h has used cells [0, a'] + [b', m_h]; its bracket k is {a', b'-1} or {a'+1, b'}; a shift
matching the runs has s = a - a' and b = b' + s, so the shifted bracket is {a, b-1} or {a+1, b} and
the rest continues as in h.  QED
Check: check_BD.py (63 staircases with <= 6 edges; 2,604 P-products and 13,908 T-products, all
staircases); closure "C P T" reaches exactly the caterpillars for n <= 14 (logs/CPT.log:
10, 20, 36, 72, 136, 272, 528, 1056 trees, 2^(n-2) placements).

5.3  The closure, and the tape graph.

Definition.  Start from all staircases of every size.  Apply the rules switched on until nothing
new appears, size by size (C within a size; T with h of the same or a smaller size; P with g
smaller; B, D from p to (p-1)t+1 vertices; Lemma 8 from n to n+1; N from n to n+g with h derived).

Theorem G (tape graph; STATUS: Proved; [added]).  Fix n.  Let Gamma be the graph with nodes (k, F),
0 <= k <= m-1, F a k-subset of {1..m-1}, and an arrow (k, F, p) from (k, F) to (k-1, F - {p, p+k})
whenever exactly one of p, p+k lies in F (0 <= p <= m-k).  Growth placements on n vertices are
exactly the paths from the source (m-1, {1..m-1}) to the sink (0, {}): the node passed at level k is
(k, Free_k), the arrow is the bracket of length k.  For a growth placement h on n' <= n vertices, an
integer s and 1 <= i <= min(m', m) - 1 (and i <= m-1) such that every bracket of h of length <= i,
shifted by s, lies in 0..m and Free_i(h) + s avoids 0 and m, call (i, Free_i(h)+s, p_i(h)+s) a
SHIFTED ARROW of h.  Let X be a non-empty set of growth placements on n vertices and D' a set of growth
placements on fewer vertices.  Let A* be the smallest set of arrows that contains
   (a) the arrows of the members of X,        (b) the shifted arrows of the members of D',
   (c) the shifted arrows of every source-sink path all of whose arrows are in A*,
   (d) with every arrow (k, F, p) its mirror (k, m - F, m-k-p).
Then the smallest set S of growth placements on n vertices that contains X and is closed under
Rule C and under Rule T (with h in S or in D') is the set of ALL source-sink paths with arrows in A*.
Proof.  By Remark T1, Rule T applies to (f, h, k, s) iff Free_k(f) = Free_k(h)+s and the arrows
(i, Free_i(h)+s, p_i(h)+s), i = k..1, are shifted arrows of h; its product is the path that follows
f down to the node (k, Free_k(f)) and then those arrows.  Rule C maps a path to the path of mirror
arrows.
 (S is contained in Paths(A*).)  Paths(A*) contains X; it is closed under C by (d); and a T-product
of f in Paths(A*) with h in Paths(A*) or D' uses arrows of f and shifted arrows of h, all in A* by
(b), (c).
 (Paths(A*) is contained in S.)  Call an arrow alpha at node (k, F) GOOD if for every f in S with
Free_k(f) = F there is f' in S that agrees with f on the brackets longer than k and whose bracket k
is alpha.  If all arrows of A* are good then every path pi in Paths(A*) is in S: all placements
share the top bracket, and if f_k in S agrees with pi above k, goodness of pi's arrow at level k
gives f_{k-1} in S agreeing with pi above k-1; f_0 = pi.  A* is the union of stages A_0 (a, b),
A_{r+1} = A_r + (c applied to Paths(A_r)) + (d applied to A_r); we show by induction on r that all
arrows of A_r are good (hence Paths(A_r) is in S).  (a): if x in X uses alpha at (k, F) and f in S
passes (k, F), then T(f, x, k, 0) is in S and is the required f'.  (b), (c): if alpha is the
shifted arrow (i, Free_i(h)+s, .) of h in D', or of h in Paths(A_r), which lies in S by induction,
and f in S passes that node, then T(f, h, i, s) is allowed (Remark T1) and is the required f'.
(d): if alpha is good and f in S passes the node of the mirror arrow, then C(f) in S passes the
node of alpha, some f' in S follows C(f) and then alpha, and C(f') follows f and then the mirror
arrow.  QED
Picture: a derived placement is a walk down the tape graph; Rule T says "two walks that meet at a
node may swap tails", so the closure is "all walks along arrows that have been seen", and the only
bookkeeping is which arrows have been seen (2^24 bits at n = 16).

(with T) keeps, per size, the arrow set A* and computes it by iterating (a)-(d); seeds from B, D

6. IS THERE A RECIPE FOR A NEW INFINITE FAMILY?     ANSWER: NO (none found)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Target: trees made of k >= 3 equal branches at one vertex and nothing else (they defeat README
Theorem 13, which needs 2m+1 copies PLUS a tree S with m edges).  Call these "equal-branch trees".
A star does not count.

What each rule can do, and why it does not give them.

(a) Rules C, T, P only recombine.  T needs a second placement whose tape matches; P needs a prefix
    whose free set is one run.  Neither says where such a placement comes from.  From staircases they
    give only caterpillars (Proposition 1, Proved).  Theorem G makes the same point as a picture:
    the closure is "all walks along arrows already seen", so T creates new walks but no new arrows
    except by shifting.  That the closure reaches every tree for n <= 19 is an Observation about
    existence, not a recipe: it does not say which walk draws a given tree.

(b) Rule N at the root.  Lemma N1 (Proved): a nested piece with g <= j is a star.  Take the star
    0-1, .., 0-m.  Every cut L is clean with j = L and u = 0, so a piece U' with g edges may be nested
    at the centre at a cut L, where it occupies the labels and the lengths [L, L+g-1]; by Lemma N1 it
    is a star unless L < g.  A cut L above all pieces nested so far is again clean with j = L, u = 0
    (the brackets jumping it are the root edges to the labels >= L, and those are exactly the
    brackets of length >= L).  So if copies of a rooted tree R with e edges are nested side by side,
    one above the other, the second cut is at L >= e + 1 > g = e and the second copy must be a
    star.  Dead end, Proved for nesting side by side at the label 0; cuts below or inside an earlier
    piece, and N at other vertices, were not ruled out by argument.

(c) Rule B on a star.  The natural attempt for "d equal hubs with a leaves each" (the depth-2
    equal-branch trees; in the notation of src/attack2/comb, the cases 3 <= a <= d-3 are the ones
    with no proved rule in this repository) is: T = K_{1,d}, every leaf owns exactly one block,
    t = a + 1.
    Lemma S (Proved; [added]).  For d >= 3 the star K_{1,d} has no valid design in which every leaf
    owns exactly one block.
    Proof.  A star has only two growth placements, centre at 0 or at d (a bracket of length k at a
    centre labelled c needs c+k or c-k as its new label; if 0 < c < d both c+1 and c-1 are labels
    but only one bracket of length 1 exists); by Rule C take the centre at 0 (the complement of a product
    is the product of the complement with the mirrored design, and conditions (1), (2) are
    unchanged), so l(v) = v.  The
    centre owns no block.  Band d-1 can only be v = d, j = 0 (v - j - 1 = d-1), since j - v = d-1 has
    no solution with v >= 1.  Band d-2: v - j - 1 = d-2 means (v, j) = (d, 1) or (d-1, 0), but leaf d
    and block 0 are taken; j - v = d-2 with v >= 1 means v = 1, j = d-1, and condition (2) asks
    d-2 < l(1) = 1.  So d <= 2.  QED
    By Lemma B1 the same statement for t = 2 reads: for d >= 3 the tree "d hubs with one leaf each"
    (the spider S(2^d)) has no growth placement with centre and hubs on the even labels.

(d) Rule B on anything small.  Observation (bscan.py; p <= 8 and t = 2..5; for p <= 7 also t = 6, 7):
    among ALL Rule B products -- every growth placement of every tree on p <= 8 vertices, every one
    of the 507,272 valid designs -- there is NO equal-branch tree with k >= 3 branches.  (By Lemma B1,
    for t = 2 this says: no equal-branch tree on 2p-1 <= 15 vertices has a growth placement with all
    odd labels on leaves.)  In particular (symdesign.py, d <= 5): the spider S(2^d), d = 3, 4, 5, has
    no growth placement with a valid design in which every middle vertex owns two blocks, or every
    middle vertex and every tip one block, or every tip two blocks.  I have no proof of the general
    statement; the mechanism in the small cases is that of Lemma S: the largest bands can only be
    owned by vertices with labels near 0 or near p-1, which breaks the symmetry between branches.

(e) Rule D with the full window (the growth Delta-construction with a star).  It would give "T with
    t-1 leaves on every vertex" (generalised coronas) for every T that has a growth placement
    satisfying the mirror condition with w = p.  Observation (mirrorscan.py, p <= 12): such trees
    are rare: 2, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 6, 13 of the 3, 6, 11, 23, 47, 106, 235, 551 trees on 5..12 vertices.
    Among staircases (Observation, 3 <= m <= 12, logs/stairmirror.log) exactly two satisfy it: the
    zigzag 0-m, 1-m, 1-(m-1), 2-(m-1), ..  (a path; here l(i) equals the bound for every i) and the
    zigzag with its last step repeated (a path with two leaves at one end).  Their products are
    caterpillars.  [A first hand derivation of mine allowed more staircases; the program corrected
    it, and I state only what was computed.]

(f) Theorem 13 + Rule P.  In the placement of Theorem 13 the tree S occupies the labels 1..m with
    the lengths 1..m, i.e. the free set at time m is one run next to the root.  Rule P then lets S
    be replaced by any growth placement on m+1 vertices glued at 0 -- which is Theorem 13 again with
    another S -- or glued at the vertex labelled m+1 (one particular copy of the vertex of T
    labelled 1), which is new but lopsided.  S cannot be removed: taking S to be j copies of T
    gives j(2e+1)+1 copies for e = number of edges of T, the "towers" already in README section 15.

What the rules DO give, honestly stated.
  * Each (T, valid design) is an infinite family indexed by t (Rule B, Proved); 2,897 such families
    for p = 7.  These are one-parameter families found by a finite search, not a recipe that takes
    a tree and returns a labelling.
  * A scaling law (Lemma B1, Proved): a growth placement of a tree in which all odd labels are
    leaves multiplies -- every bunch of lambda odd leaves on a vertex may be replaced by (t-1)*lambda
    leaves.
  * For stars (centre at 0) the possible ways to hand out blocks were enumerated for d <= 9
    (starprofiles.py, logs/starprofiles.log; Observation).  The closest to "equal hubs" is: d-r leaves
    own one block each, the centre owns r blocks; that is the tree "k = d-r hubs with a = t-1 leaves
    each, plus r(a+1) leaves on the centre" for every a >= 1.  The least r is 1, 1, 1, 2, 1, 2, 2 for
    d = 3..9 (never 0, by Lemma S).  I found no formula for the design as a function of d, and did
    not check these trees against the diameter-4 results of src/attack/gamestrategy and
    src/attack2/comb, which treat the same trees with other tools; no novelty is claimed.

So: no combination of T, P, N, B, D that I tried gives a recipe for equal-branch trees, and for the
most direct attempts there is a proved or exhaustively observed reason.


7. DEAD ENDS (kept)
-------------------
  * Equal hubs from a star by Rule B: impossible for d >= 3 (Lemma S).
  * Equal branches from any Rule B product with p <= 8: none (Observation, 6(d)).
  * Copies of a non-star piece nested side by side at the root by Rule N: only the first (6(b)).
  * Full-window Rule D: applies to 13 of 551 trees at p = 12 (6(e)).
  * Removing S from Theorem 13 by Rule P or Lemma 8: S can be exchanged, not removed (6(f)).
  * A uniform design for the star with "all but r leaves own one block": least r is not monotone
    (1, 1, 1, 2, 1, 2, 2 for d = 3..9); no pattern found.
  * n = 20 for the C T B closure: not finished (stopped at the time limit); nothing is claimed for it.

9. WHAT A REFEREE SHOULD LOOK AT FIRST
--------------------------------------
  * Rule T, the case split "a in Free_k(h) or a touched" -- it is the whole proof.
  * Rule N, the sentence "the non-jumping brackets of f ... exactly as in f".
  * Rule B, the inequality t*l(v) > beta*t + t - 1  <=>  l(v) >= beta + 1.
  * Theorem G, the notion of a good arrow and case (d).  The numbers do not depend on it alone:
    the naive engine agrees for n <= 11, and the source's independent program agrees at n = 14..16.
  * The C T D entry at n = 10 (5.3): run the source's own closure.cpp.
