LINE "spare": THE ONE-SPARE-LABEL OBSERVATION
=========================================

Nothing here proves the Graceful Tree Conjecture or Conjecture B.  Status words as in CLAUDE.md.  "Proved" below
means: a complete argument is written in this file and the construction was machine-checked from the definition;
none of it has been seen by a referee yet.

CONVENTIONS
  Labels are written 0..N-1 with the root at 0 (README labels are these plus 1).  n = number of vertices.
  A relaxed growth labeling of (T,r) with range N: injective f: V -> {0..N-1}, f(r) = 0, pairwise distinct edge
  lengths |f(u)-f(v)|, lengths strictly decreasing along every path away from r.  slack = N - n.
  ONE-SPARE labeling = slack 1: labels 0..n, exactly one label and exactly one of the lengths 1..n stay unused.
  slack 0 = "r is an anchor" (README Theorem 7).  Slack s implies slack s+1 (leave the top label unused).
  CROSSING with threshold a: every edge joins a label <= a ("low") to a label > a ("high"); equivalently every
  even-depth label is below every odd-depth label.

SUMMARY
  (1) Every requested test succeeds with ONE spare label; no vertex needing slack 2 was found anywhere.
      - L(3;8,9), v (24 vertices): one-spare labeling found (no backtracking); also L(l;2l+2,2l+3), l = 1..8.
      - DH(p,q), middle vertex, every prime m = p+q+2 <= 151, all p,q >= 1 (1,160 trees).
      - H(1,1,k), every odd m = k+5 in [11,197] that rule H3 makes a non-anchor (26 trees, including
        m = 13, 73, 133, 193 = 60i+13).
      - Paths: EVERY vertex of P_n for 3 <= n <= 200 (10,098 rooted paths), plus all vertices whose two arms are
        both = 3 (mod 4) and >= 15 for n <= 331 (1,482 rooted paths).
      - S(L,L,L): every vertex (leaf included) for L <= 30 (n <= 91; 495 rooted spiders).
      - All trees, all vertices: n <= 23 (section 1; n = 20..23 are new; 341,045,702 pairs at n = 23).
      - Every centre-rooted tree of depth 2 with n <= 30 (23,023 rooted trees).
  (2) The reason, for the arithmetic families, is now PROVED and is one picture (section 2): the exact rules H2/H3
      come from a chain "this length can only go there, so the next one can only go there, ..." that must close up,
      which forces a divisibility.  One spare label cuts the chain once.  Two hubs can then sit on NEIGHBOURING
      labels and each take a block of consecutive lengths; the two blocks of leaf labels are offset by one cell;
      the unused LENGTH is the seam between the blocks and the unused LABEL is the cell the offset block cannot
      reach.  Theorems G, S, W, P below make this exact:
        Theorem S (sidestep)  => every DH(p,q) and every L(l;h1,h2), all parameters.
        Theorem W (window)    => every DH(p,q), every H(1,1,k), and every depth-2 root whose hub sizes satisfy (C).
        Theorem P (paths)     => every vertex of every path, except possibly when both arms are = 3 (mod 4) and >= 19.
  (3) Conjecture S1 (every vertex of every tree has a one-spare labeling) is formulated in section 3 with a
      stronger leaf form S1+.  I found NO implication from S1 to Conjecture B and explain why (section 3).

2. THE REASON: FOUR THEOREMS  (Proved: full arguments here; machine-checked by check_thms.py; not yet refereed)

THEOREM G (gluing keeps the spare).
  Let (O,r) have a relaxed growth labeling g with labels in 0..R and slack s, crossing with threshold a.  Let y be
  a vertex of O that is INNERMOST on its side: g(y) is the least used label > a (high case) or the greatest used
  label <= a (low case).  Let T'' be a tree with q edges and a growth placement tau: V(T'') -> {0..q} with
  tau(y'') = 0 (y'' is an anchor of T'').  Let T be O and T'' glued by identifying y with y''.
  Then (T,r) has a relaxed growth labeling with labels in 0..R+q, i.e. with the same slack s.  Its unused lengths
  are the unused lengths of g raised by q; its unused labels are those of g (low ones unchanged, high ones + q).
  Proof.  Put f(z) = g(z) if g(z) <= a and f(z) = g(z)+q if g(z) > a, for z in O.  For z in T'' put
  f(z) = g(y)+q-tau(z) in the high case and f(z) = g(y)+tau(z) in the low case; both agree with f(y).
  Labels: T'' fills the block [g(y), g(y)+q] (q+1 consecutive cells).  High case: every other high label of O is
  > g(y), so after the shift it is > g(y)+q; low labels are <= a < g(y).  Low case: every other low label is
  < g(y); high labels are >= a+1+q > g(y)+q.  So f is injective, with values in 0..R+q.
  Lengths: every edge of O crosses the threshold, so its length grows by exactly q: the O-lengths stay distinct,
  keep their order, and are all >= q+1.  T'' uses the lengths 1..q once each.
  Decrease: inside O nothing changed in the order; inside T'' lengths decrease away from y; a path from r that
  enters T'' does so through y, and if y != r the edge above y is an O-edge, longer than q.
  Count: |V(T)| = |V(O)|+q and the range grew by q, so the slack is s.  []
  (For s = 0 this is the gluing statement of README section 7.  Picture: cut the number line between low and
  high, pull the halves q cells apart, drop T'' into the gap with its anchor on the cell next to the cut.)

THEOREM S (sidestep: the neighbour of the innermost vertex may carry leaves, at the price of one label).
  Let (O,r) have a growth placement g: V(O) -> {0..R} (bijective, slack 0), crossing with threshold a.
  Low version (a >= 1): x, y the vertices with g(x) = a-1, g(y) = a.
  High version (a <= R-2): x, y the vertices with g(x) = a+2, g(y) = a+1.
  Let T'' have q >= 0 edges and a growth placement tau with tau(y'') = 0, and let h >= 0.  Let T be O, with T''
  glued at y = y'' and h new leaves attached to x.  (n = R+1+q+h vertices.)
  Then (T,r) has a one-spare labeling.  With D = q+h+1:
     O:      f = g on labels <= a,  f = g + D on labels > a;
     T'':    f = a + tau                 (low)        f = a+1+D - tau              (high)
     the i-th new leaf of x (i = 1..h) has length q+1+i:
             f = (a-1) + (q+1+i) = a+q+i (low)        f = (a+2+D) - (q+1+i) = a+1+D-q-i   (high)
     unused length: q+1.     unused label: a+D (low),  a+1 (high).
  Proof (low version; the high one is its mirror image inside the gap).  Labels: O-low 0..a; T'' fills a..a+q
  (sharing y at a); the new leaves fill a+q+1..a+q+h; a+q+h+1 = a+D is unused; O-high fills a+1+D..R+D = n.
  Lengths: T'' uses 1..q; q+1 is unused; the new leaves use q+2..q+h+1 = D; the O-edges cross, so they use
  D+1..D+R.  All distinct.  Decrease: O-edges keep their order; an edge of T'' or a new leaf edge has length <= D
  and the edge above y resp. x (if y resp. x is not r) is an O-edge of length >= D+1; inside T'' lengths decrease
  away from y.  n vertices on labels 0..n: slack 1.  []
  Picture (low version):
        0 ... a-1   a | a+1 ........ a+q | a+q+1 ..... a+q+h | a+D | a+D+1 ......... n
        O low    x   y |   rest of T''    |  leaves of x      | --  |   O high (moved D cells right)
  The hub x is one cell LEFT of where an exact hub would stand, so its fan of leaves (lengths q+2..D, consecutive)
  ends one cell short of the high block: the cell a+D stays empty.  The length q+1 is the seam: from x it would
  land on a+q, from y on a+q+1, both taken.
  Which O exist: staircases (README Theorem 2) are crossing growth placements, so O may be any caterpillar in a
  staircase, with x, y the two innermost labels of one side.
  Machine check:  python3 check_thms.py S 9 6 6  -> 119,476 labelings (every staircase with R <= 9, both versions,
  q <= 6 with T'' a star, a zigzag path or a broom, h <= 6) built and verified from the definition.

THEOREM W (window; roots of depth-2 trees).
  Let the root v have r children x_0, .., x_{r-1}; x_j has a_j >= 0 leaf children and there are no other
  vertices (a_j = 0: x_j is a leaf of the root).  Order them so that a_0 >= a_1 >= .. >= a_{r-1}.  Put
  K = 1 + a_0 + .. + a_{r-1} and d = a_1 + 2 a_2 + .. + (r-1) a_{r-1}.  Suppose
        (C)      a_2 + 2 a_3 + .. + (r-2) a_{r-1}  <=  a_0            (equivalently d <= K-1).
  Then (T,v) has a one-spare labeling; moreover the unused length s may be ANY number in 1..K-d, and the unused
  label is then K+1-s-d.
  Construction (n = K+r vertices, labels 0..K+r).  f(v) = 0, f(x_j) = K+1+j: the hubs stand on the top r cells,
  the biggest hub lowest.  Every leaf hangs DOWN from its hub.  Cut the lengths s+1..s+d (the window) into
  consecutive blocks, a_j blocks of size j for each j >= 1.  In a block p+1..p+j the top length p+j goes to x_j
  (leaf at K+1+j-(p+j) = K+1-p) and the j-1 lengths p+1..p+j-1 go to x_0 (leaf at K+1-w).  Every length w
  outside the window except s goes to x_0 (leaf at K+1-w).  Length s is unused.
  Proof.  Write a leaf label as K+1-t.  A length w given to x_0 has t = w; the top of a block has t = p.  So a
  block p+1..p+j is mapped onto the t-values p, p+1, .., p+j-1 bijectively (top to bottom, the rest fixed), the
  window s+1..s+d is mapped bijectively onto s..s+d-1, and the lengths outside the window keep t = w.  Hence
  the lengths 1..K except s are mapped bijectively onto the t-values 1..K except s+d: the leaf labels are
  distinct, lie in 1..K (below all hubs), and miss exactly K+1-s-d.  Counts: x_j (j >= 1) gets one length per
  block of size j, a_j in all; x_0 gets the remaining K-1-(a_1+..+a_{r-1}) = a_0.  The window fits because
  s+d <= K.  Lengths: hubs use K+1..K+r, leaves use 1..K except s, so all are distinct and each leaf edge is
  shorter than the edge above its hub.  n = K+r vertices on labels 0..K+r.  []
  Picture: with exact labels the leaf labels K+1-w would simply mirror the lengths (all leaves on x_0).  Each
  leaf moved to the hub j cells higher has to use a length j larger to reach the same row of cells; inside the
  window every such jump "t = w - j" is paid for by j-1 untouched cells it leaps over, and the whole window
  slides down by one cell.  The cell it vacates at the top (t = s+d) is the unused label; the length it never
  uses at the bottom (s) is the unused length.
  Machine check:  python3 check_thms.py W 30 6  -> all 10,707 hub-size vectors with <= 6 children, <= 30 leaves
  satisfying (C), every admissible s: 100,007 labelings verified from the definition.
  COROLLARY W1.  DH(p,q) again (r = 2: (C) is empty).  H(1,1,k) for EVERY k >= 1: (a_0,a_1,a_2) = (k,1,1), (C)
   reads 1 <= k, and s may be anything in 1..k.  Explicitly for s = 1 (labels 0..k+6, K = k+3, d = 3):
        v = 0;  big hub at k+4, small hubs at k+5 and k+6;  leaf of k+5 at k+3 (length 2), leaf of k+6 at k+2
        (length 4, the block {3,4}), big-hub leaves at k+1 (length 3) and at k+4-w for w = 5..k+3;
        unused length 1, unused label k.
   So rule H3 (3 | m or 5 | m or m = 3 mod 4) also disappears with one spare label, for every k and not only for
   m = 60i+13.  More generally any number of root leaves can be added for free (a_j = 0 costs nothing in (C)),
   and for sizes a_0 >= a_1 >= a_2 followed by zeros (C) reads a_2 <= a_0, which always holds: EVERY root with
   at most three non-leaf children in a depth-2 tree has a one-spare labeling.
  Not covered by W: e.g. four hubs of sizes (3,3,3,3).  Of the 23,023 centre-rooted depth-2 trees with n <= 30,
  10,932 satisfy (C); the others were settled by search (section 1).
  REMARK (why (C) is exactly the limit of THIS shape; argument sketched, not refereed).  If the hubs stand on the
  top r cells, a leaf cannot hang upward, so every leaf has t = w - j with j >= 0, where j is the rank of its hub
  from the bottom.  Comparing sums, (unused label's t) - (unused length) = sum of all j; lengths below s are
  forced to t = w, lengths above the hole too, and the window in between has d elements with total shift d.
  So the number of j = 0 leaves inside the window is sum (j-1) c_j, which needs (C) for some ordering of the
  hubs.  Other hub positions are needed for the remaining trees.

THEOREM P (paths).
  Let the root of a path have arms of b and c edges (b + c + 1 = n vertices).  It has a one-spare labeling
  unless possibly  b = c = 3 (mod 4) and min(b,c) >= 19.   (So: every vertex of P_n when n is not 3 mod 4 or
  n < 39; the vertex at distance 2 from an end for every n; the three middle vertices of P_n for every n, except
  the exact middle (arms (c,c)) when c = 3 mod 4 and c >= 19, i.e. n = 8u+7 with u >= 4.)
  Proof.  b = 0 or c = 0: zigzag 0, n-1, 1, n-2, .. (slack 0).  b = 1: put the leaf at n-1 and run the zigzag
  0, n-2, 1, n-3, .. along the other arm: lengths n-1; n-2, n-3, .., 1 (slack 0).
  Otherwise use a PIECE: a crossing one-spare labeling of the path with arms (b, j), j in {1,2,3,7}, in which the
  tip y of the j-arm is innermost on its side; Theorem G with T'' = zigzag path with q edges anchored at its end
  then gives arms (b, j+q) for every q >= 0.  Pieces (A = labels along the b-arm from the root, B = along the
  j-arm; R = top label = b+j+1; "zigzag" = alternately the next unused high cell and the next unused low cell):
   P2  b = 4t+2, t >= 0, j = 1, R = 4t+4.   A: zigzag 0, R, 1, R-1, .., for 2t+2 edges (lengths R, .., 2t+3; it
       stands on the low cell t+1; highs used R..3t+4).  SKIP the high cell 3t+3.  Continue the zigzag for 2t
       edges: 3t+2, t+2, 3t+1, t+3, .., (lengths 2t+1, .., 2; highs 3t+2..2t+3, lows t+2..2t+1).
       B: 0 -> y = 2t+2 (length 2t+2, exactly the length the skipped step would have used).
       Lows 0..2t+1, y = 2t+2 is the least high label; unused label 3t+3, unused length 1.
   P1  b = 4t+1, t >= 1, j = 1, R = 4t+3.   A: zigzag for 2t+2 edges (lengths R, .., 2t+2; on low cell t+1;
       highs R..3t+3).  SKIP the high cell 3t+2.  Continue for 2t-1 edges: 3t+1, t+2, 3t, .., (lengths 2t, .., 2;
       highs 3t+1..2t+2, lows t+2..2t).   B: 0 -> y = 2t+1 (length 2t+1).
       Lows 0..2t, y least high; unused label 3t+2, unused length 1.
   P3  b = 4t, t >= 1, j = 2, R = 4t+3.     B: 0 -> R -> y = 2t+2 (lengths R and 2t+1).
       A: zigzag 0, R-1, 1, R-2, .. for 2t+1 edges (lengths R-1, .., 2t+2; on the high cell 3t+2; lows used
       1..t).  SKIP the low cell t+1.  Continue for 2t-1 edges: t+2, 3t+1, t+3, .., (lengths 2t, .., 2; lows
       t+2..2t+1, highs 3t+1..2t+3).   y = 2t+2 is the greatest low label; unused label t+1, unused length 1.
   In each case the lengths along A are R (or R-1), R-1, .. down to the skip, then continue one lower than they
   would have, because after skipping a cell the zigzag is one cell shorter: the missing length (2t+2, 2t+1,
   2t+1 respectively) is the one given to y.  Checking the three cases is the arithmetic in brackets: each step
   of a zigzag joins the current cell to the far end of the remaining interval, so consecutive lengths drop by
   one; all low cells are below all high cells; A decreases; B decreases (R > 2t+1 in P3).
   Sporadic pieces (found by armpieces.py / armfast.py, verified by check_thms.py):
       b = 3,  j = 3:  A = 0,7,1,6                      B = 0,4,2,3
       b = 7,  j = 3:  A = 0,11,1,10,2,9,4,8            B = 0,6,3,5
       b = 11, j = 7:  A = 0,19,1,18,2,17,4,16,6,15,8,12   B = 0,14,3,11,5,10,7,9
       b = 15, j = 3:  A = 0,19,1,18,2,17,3,16,5,15,6,13,7,11,8,10   B = 0,12,4,9

3. THE CONJECTURE, AND WHAT IT WOULD (NOT) SAY ABOUT CONJECTURE B
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONJECTURE S1 (one spare label, every root).  For every tree T on n vertices and every vertex r there is an
  injective labeling of V(T) by 1..n+1 with r at 1, pairwise distinct edge lengths, and lengths strictly
  decreasing along every path away from r.
  Evidence: all trees with n <= 23, all vertices (section 1); the families of section 1; Proved for the middle
  vertex of every DH(p,q), for v in every L(l;h1,h2), for every depth-2 root satisfying (C) (in particular
  H(1,1,k) and every root with at most three non-leaf children in a depth-2 tree), and for path vertices as in
  Theorem P.  It is closed under the two standard growth operations (both Proved, trivial):
   (a) adding a leaf at the root (give it the new top label; README Lemma 6 with a spare);
   (b) Theorem G gluing (crossing pieces).
  Also: if l is a leaf with neighbour v and (T - l, v) has a one-spare labeling, so has (T, l) (put l at 0, v at
  the top, the rest reflected below it).  So S1 for internal vertices implies S1 for leaves.

OBSERVATION S1+ (leaf form; all trees with n <= 15).  For every non-anchor pair (T,r) there is a vertex w such
  that r is an anchor of T + (one new leaf at w).  [python3 extras.py E1 n, n = 6..15: non-anchor pairs
  1, 4, 6, 11, 19, 21, 37, 54, 84, 117; in every case some w works.  w = r itself works in 1, 4, 5, 10, 16, 21,
  37, 50, 82, 116 of them, so "add the leaf at r" FAILS first at n = 8 (i.e. on a 9-vertex tree), as the
  asymptotic line already noted.]
  S1+ implies S1 (delete the new leaf: one label and one length become unused) and says more: the unused label u
  and unused length k can be chosen so that u = f(w) +- k with k shorter than the edge above w - the spare cell
  is a socket into which a real leaf fits.  For Theorem S the socket exists when h >= q+1 (the leaf of x with
  length h+1 sits at a+h = (a+D) - (q+1)).

DOES S1 SAY ANYTHING ABOUT CONJECTURE B?  I found no implication in either direction, and here is what blocks it.
  * B for T needs an EXACT placement.  The only exact statement obtainable from a one-spare labeling is by filling
    the hole: a one-spare labeling of (T', r) with unused label u and unused length k extends to a growth
    placement of T' + leaf at w exactly when w sits at u +- k and k is shorter than the edge above w (or w = r
    and u = k).  S1 gives no control over (u, k); S1+ gives some w, not a prescribed one.
  * Bridge (Proved, trivial).  T has a growth placement in which the bracket of length 1 hangs the leaf x on y
    if and only if T - x has a one-spare labeling (some root) whose unused length is 1 and whose unused label is
    f(y) +- 1.  (Delete or re-insert x; a bracket of length 1 is always last.)  So Conjecture B for T is the
    statement "for SOME leaf x the hole can be steered next to x's neighbour with the seam at length 1".
  * FAILS (smallest counterexample n = 6): "for EVERY leaf x there is a growth placement whose length-1 bracket
    hangs x".  In S(2,2,1) no growth placement puts length 1 on the short leg (all 12 growth placements put it
    at the end of a long leg; brute force over 720 labelings, and extras.py E2 6).  Counts of failing (tree,
    leaf) pairs for n = 4..10: 0, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 11.  So the natural uniform strengthening that WOULD make S1-type
    statements talk to B is false, and an induction "delete a leaf, apply S1, put it back" has no handle on which
    leaf.
  * What S1+ would give is only: every tree is one leaf away from a tree with a growth placement anchored at any
    prescribed vertex.  That is an embedding statement, not B.
  * In the other direction B (some anchor) says nothing about every root.
  * S1 would imply the much weaker classical-type statement "every tree has an injective labeling in 0..n with
    distinct edge lengths" (a range-relaxed graceful labeling with one spare label).  I have NOT checked the
    literature for the status of that statement (Van Bussel 2002 on relaxed graceful labelings is the place to
    look); no novelty is claimed for S1 or for Theorems G, S, W, P.
  Honest summary: S1 explains why anchors fail (exactness forces closed chains, hence arithmetic) and shows the
  obstruction is exactly one cell wide in every case examined, but it does not reduce Conjecture B.

4. DEAD ENDS AND CAVEATS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. A bug worth recording: my first search restored its "active parent" list with pop_back after recursion,
    which is wrong when deeper calls reorder the list; it reported false NONE for path middles from n = 10.
    Fixed (the list is restored from a snapshot); the slack-0 counts then reproduce the known non-anchor counts
    for n = 6..18, and every FOUND is checked twice from the definition.  NONE at slack 1 never occurs anywhere.
 2. Paths with both arms = 3 (mod 4): no crossing piece with a short second arm exists for b = 11 below j = 7
    or for b = 19 below j = 7 (exhaustive), and I found no uniform family; arms both >= 19 are open (Observation
    to n = 331 only).  A general proof for paths probably needs the two arms treated together, not piece + zigzag.
 3. Theorem W does not cover all depth-2 roots (four equal hubs of size 3 already violate (C)); hubs must then
    leave the top cells.  No general depth-2 theorem was obtained.
 4. The plain longest-first search stalls on centre-rooted depth-2 trees with many small hubs for n >= 25 (e.g.
    twelve hubs with one leaf each); randomised parent order with restarts (portfolio.sh) settles them.
 5. I looked for a way to make two spare cells cancel (glue two one-spare pieces so that each fills the other's
    hole).  Theorem G only ADDS slacks; a cancelling composition would need the O-part to supply a vertex in the
    hole of T'' joined by an edge of exactly the missing length, and I found no general way to arrange that.
 6. No single position of the unused label or length works across families: in Theorem S the unused length is
    q+1 (the seam), in Theorem W it can be anything in 1..K-d, in the path pieces it is 1 before gluing.
